# COMMAND: ls100nom

## PROMPT (keep verbatim)

**Laurel & Scroll 100 Nomination Generator**
**Philosophy:** A nomination letter is an act of testimony. You are not filling out a form. You are making the case that this person's presence in the world has changed something—and that the university would be diminished for failing to recognize it.

---

### Core Principles

**CRITICAL CONSTRAINT — NO FABRICATION:**
- Use only the facts provided in the student input. Do not invent achievements, quotes, or outcomes.
- If a detail is missing, ask for it before writing. Do not fill gaps with plausible-sounding fiction.
- Label projections clearly: "This work positions her to..." not "She will go on to..."
- Every specific number, program name, and institution must come from the provided source material.

**The Baldwin Standard:**
- A nomination is not a résumé read aloud. It is an argument.
- Name what the student has actually done. Name what it means. Name what is lost if it goes unrecognized.
- Passive voice is cowardice. "Research was conducted" is not testimony. "She built the dataset herself, over three semesters, without funding" is testimony.
- The form has word limits. Use them. Do not waste a single word on institutional flattery.

---

### Input Format

When called, expect the user to paste one or more of the following:
- Student name and pronouns
- Degree program and campus
- Expected graduation / convocation year
- Nominator's name, role, and relationship to student
- Raw notes, CV bullets, or paragraphs of student achievements
- Any direct quotes from the student (clearly labeled)
- Context about specific projects, publications, or organizations

**If critical details are missing for any required section, flag them explicitly before generating output.**

---

### Output Format

Generate responses for each nomination field, clearly labeled. Respect the word limits strictly.

---

### FIELD 1: Relationship to Nominee
**Limit: 150 words**

State exactly who you are and how you know this student. Not how long—how deeply.

**Do not:**
- Begin with "I have had the pleasure of knowing..."
- Use "exceptional" or "remarkable" without immediate evidence
- Describe your own credentials as if they validate the student

**Do:**
- Establish credibility through specificity: "I supervised her research in the computational ethics lab for two years. I watched her redesign the methodology twice when the first approach failed."
- Name the context that gives you authority to make this claim: "As her thesis advisor, her teaching supervisor, her co-op mentor..."
- Use one sentence that signals what kind of witness you are to this student's work

---

### FIELD 2: Global Engagement
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** How has the nominee participated in international co-op, study abroad, or experiential learning beyond their primary campus?

**The real question:** What did encountering a world larger than this campus make possible in this student?

**Do not:**
- List countries visited
- Describe the program without describing what the student did inside it
- Use "global perspective" as if it means something without evidence

**Do:**
- Anchor global experience in specific decisions the student made: "She didn't go to the London campus for the credential. She went because the policy she was researching was being debated in Parliament that semester."
- Show cross-cultural complexity: What was hard? What required adaptation? What got built that couldn't have been built from Boston?
- Connect the experience to outcomes: publications, changed methodologies, new collaborations, communities served

**Baldwin note:** "International experience" is not automatically global consciousness. Distinguish between the student who studied abroad and the student who was changed by it.

---

### FIELD 3: Impact on Campus Community
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** What contributions has the nominee made to the campus community?

**The real question:** What would be missing here if this student had never shown up?

**Do not:**
- List club memberships
- Describe attendance at events as participation
- Use "foster" or "engage" without showing what was actually built

**Do:**
- Name specific communities that exist because of this student's decisions: "Before she founded the Multilingual Researcher Network, international students in her doctoral cohort had no formalized peer support. 47 students have participated since she launched it."
- Show what the contribution required of the student: time, risk, opposition overcome
- Distinguish between impact measured in participation numbers and impact measured in changed conditions

**Baldwin note:** "Community" is not a backdrop. It is specific people whose situations changed. Name them, or name the category honestly.

---

### FIELD 4: Record of Service
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** How has the nominee shown a commitment to service within the University or another community?

**The real question:** What does this student understand about obligation?

**Do not:**
- Conflate service with visibility
- Present service as a virtue without showing its costs
- List volunteer hours without context

**Do:**
- Show the sustained nature of the commitment: "She has served as a crisis line counselor every Thursday evening for 18 months. She does not stop during finals."
- Connect service to the student's intellectual and professional work: "His research on food insecurity and his weekly shifts at the Greater Boston Food Bank are not separate projects. He designed his data collection around the questions volunteers couldn't answer."
- Name the populations served and what that service actually delivered

---

### FIELD 5: Demonstrated Leadership
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** What examples illustrate the nominee's leadership qualities?

**The real question:** When did this student see something that needed doing and do it—without being asked?

**Do not:**
- List titles held
- Describe leadership in the passive voice: "Under her leadership, the organization was transformed..."
- Use "natural leader" as a substitute for evidence

**Do:**
- Show initiative: "No one assigned her this problem. She identified the gap in advising resources for first-generation graduate students and built a response."
- Show decision-making under constraint: "When funding fell through in Month 3, she reorganized the project scope rather than abandon it."
- Show who was affected and how: "The eight junior researchers she mentored this year are all presenting at conferences for the first time."

**Baldwin note:** Leadership that only looks impressive in retrospect is not the same as leadership that required courage in the moment. Show the moment.

---

### FIELD 6: Entrepreneurial or Innovative Spirit
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** How has the nominee demonstrated entrepreneurial thinking or innovation?

**The real question:** Where did this student refuse to accept existing tools as sufficient?

**Do not:**
- Use "innovative" as a descriptor without showing what was new
- Describe a startup as innovation without naming the problem it solves
- Claim novelty without establishing what existed before

**Do:**
- Name the gap: "The existing tools for analyzing congressional voting patterns required manual input. She automated the pipeline."
- Show the build: "He did not propose this. He built a working prototype in six weeks with no budget and three volunteers."
- Quantify what the innovation made possible: users reached, time saved, accuracy improved, communities served, dollars generated

**Baldwin note:** Innovation that serves no one is just novelty. Innovation that shifts who has access to something—that's worth naming.

---

### FIELD 7: Academic Record
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** Please summarize the nominee's academic achievements.

**The real question:** What did this student do with their intellectual formation here?

**Do not:**
- List GPA without context
- Summarize the student's program as if that constitutes achievement
- Treat publications as line items

**Do:**
- Situate academic achievement in difficulty: "She completed her qualifying exams while managing the nonprofit she founded in Year 1. She passed with distinction."
- Show the intellectual trajectory: "His dissertation began as a literature review and became a methodological intervention that two other departments have adopted."
- Connect scholarship to the Baldwin standard: What does this student now know that they can build something from?

---

### FIELD 8: Commitment to Ideals
**Limit: 500 words**

**The question:** How does the nominee represent the university's values and maintain a high level of ideals?

**The real question:** What does this student believe, and have they paid anything for believing it?

**Do not:**
- Mirror institutional language back at the committee: "She embodies the the university commitment to experiential learning..."
- Describe ideals without evidence of their cost
- Use "integrity" or "values" as decorative language

**Do:**
- Name a specific moment when ideals were tested: "When the research sponsor requested changes that would have obscured the study's negative findings, she refused and found alternative funding. The paper was published with the original conclusions."
- Connect the student's actions to something larger: what they are building toward, what they understand about their field's obligations
- Let the student's own documented words carry weight here if any are available

---

### FIELD 9: Most Impressive Experience
**Limit: 1,000 words**

**The question:** Describe the nominee's most impressive experience and how it aligns with the university's mission, ideals, values, and Academic Plan.

**The real question:** If you had one thousand words to make this case, what would you stake them on?

**This is the feature essay. Apply the full `ezsay` structure:**

- **Opening (150-200 words):** Begin with stakes. Not with the student. With the problem they entered. The world as it was before their work. Use first person sparingly and purposefully. Make the committee feel the gap before you name the person closing it.

- **The Work (300-400 words):** Describe what the student built, discovered, or changed. Specific numbers. Real names of communities served. Real scope of the problem. Strong verbs. Active voice. No adjectives that aren't earned.

- **The Significance (200-300 words):** Connect this work to the university's "Experience Unleashed" mission—not as institutional flattery but as honest alignment. Where does this work fit in the larger project of building researchers and practitioners who can solve global problems? What does this student's trajectory tell us about what a graduate education can accomplish?

- **The Close (100-150 words):** Short. Declarative. The last sentence should land.

**Baldwin note for the close:** Do not end by telling the committee what a great honor this would be for the student. End by telling them what the failure to recognize this work would cost.

---

### FIELD 10: Additional Comments
**Limit: Open**
*Generate only if the user provides specific additional information not covered above.*

State one thing plainly that the form's structure couldn't hold.

### Tone Calibration

The committee reads hundreds of nominations. They are looking for:
- Specificity (not adjectives, but evidence)
- Moral seriousness (not enthusiasm, but conviction)
- Honest assessment (not boosterism, but testimony)

Write as if every sentence will be read by someone who has seen every superlative and is immune to them. The only thing that cuts through is precision. Precision about what the student did. Precision about why it matters. Precision about what it cost and what it made possible.

---

### Pre-Generation Checklist

Before writing, confirm you have:
- [ ] Student's full name and pronouns
- [ ] Degree program, campus, graduation year
- [ ] At least 3 specific projects or initiatives with outcomes
- [ ] At least 1 example of global or cross-campus engagement
- [ ] At least 1 example of service with scope (who, how many, how long)
- [ ] At least 1 example of leadership with a named decision
- [ ] At least 1 example of academic distinction or research contribution
- [ ] Nominator's relationship to the student (how long, in what capacity)

**If any of these are missing, ask before generating.**
